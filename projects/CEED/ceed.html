The Exam Is Accessible. Is the Journey to It?

First, we audited 15 Indian competitive examination portals in a joint accessibility research paper—then carried those findings directly into an inclusive redesign of the CEED website.

Team Rehan Jose & Malavika P Pillai
Context NIFT Kannur · Inclusive Design
Status Ongoing · Research & CEED Discovery
Methods WAVE Audit, Manual WCAG 2.2, Survey & UX Redesign

Part 01 · Research Paper

Accessibility & Inclusivity of Competitive Examination Registration Portals in India

Competitive examinations are a primary gateway to higher education, professional licensing, and public employment in India. While conducting bodies increasingly provide accommodations inside the exam hall—such as scribes, extra time, and accessible centres—those measures break down if a candidate with disabilities cannot independently navigate the website to discover the exam, understand eligibility, request provisions, and submit the application.

Together with my teammate Malavika P Pillai, we set out to investigate a focused question: Can a candidate independently discover, navigate, understand, and complete the digital registration journey for an Indian competitive examination using assistive or alternative forms of interaction?

15 Major Indian exam portals audited across the 2026 registration cycle
2-Layer Automated WAVE scans paired with manual keyboard, screen-reader & voice walkthroughs
5K–52L+ Annual candidate scale—from specialized design tests to massive national exams

We intentionally evaluated portals across the full spectrum of candidate participation—from high-volume national recruitment and entrance systems to specialized design, architecture, and law examinations:

RRB Group D SSC CGL NEET UG NTA / CUET UG GATE UPSC CSE JEE Advanced CAT KEAM CLAT ICAI NATA ICAR NID DAT CEED ★

Accessibility Is a Journey, Not a Feature

One of our central findings was that a low automated WAVE error count—or an accessibility toolbar on the homepage—does not guarantee a candidate can finish applying. On portals like UPSC and RRB, accessibility panels present on the main site vanished the moment a candidate entered the registration portal itself. Barriers appeared across every step of the flow:

Discover → Understand → Navigate → Register → Verify → Upload → Submit
01

Technical Accessibility

Missing alternative text, contrast failures, structural and ARIA defects, and unlabeled form controls detected through WAVE—revealing wide inconsistency with no shared accessibility pattern across Indian exam systems.

02

Structural Navigability

Keyboard focus that disappeared midway through pages, auto-opening dropdowns forcing users through every menu item, inaccessible pop-ups and OTP inputs, broken mobile ordering, and accessibility controls dropping off during registration and payment.

03

Information Accessibility

Critical brochures, eligibility rules, and PwD provisions locked inside untagged PDFs; auto-scrolling tickers carrying dates and scribe notices; vague labels like “Self-Service Portal” or “CEN”; and generic “click here” upload links.

“An accessible examination does not necessarily mean an accessible path to the examination.”

Part 02 · Applied Redesign (Ongoing)

From Research to Action: Redesigning the CEED Website

After completing the 15-portal study, we chose to focus our redesign on CEED (Common Entrance Examination for Design)—India’s premier entrance examination for postgraduate design programmes at IITs and IISc. Despite representing the country’s design community, CEED recorded the second-highest WAVE error count (45 errors, 118 alerts, AIM score 3.5) among all 15 audited portals.

Our manual audit exposed no accessibility panel, no search or sitemap, invisible keyboard focus with auto-opening dropdowns, an untagged PDF brochure opening inside the same tab, generic “click here” upload links, and help delivered primarily through images. Below is our ongoing CEED redesign work-in-progress deck—covering heuristic evaluation, WCAG mapping, stakeholder prioritization, candidate survey insights, affinity mapping, personas, empathy maps, and user scenarios.

What’s Next: This project is actively in progress. With our 15-portal research paper, CEED audit, survey synthesis, personas, and storyboards complete, we are now moving into restructured information architecture, accessible wireframes, and a tested interactive prototype for CEED.
